NOT MAGIC. JUST A BETTER QUESTION.

Don’t fill a line.
Compose a paragraph.

Instead of asking “does one more word fit?”, we ask “which breaks make the whole thing feel better?” There isn’t one perfect answer. These controls let you decide what better means.

01

Measure the possibilities

Measure candidate lines in the chosen font. Keep words whole and allow only a small, bounded change in letter-spacing.

02

Look beyond the next line

A k-best dynamic program weighs right-edge slack, short endings, tracking, and abrupt spacing changes across each paragraph.

03

Bring the paragraphs together

Compare the retained layouts jointly. Reward similar body contours and endings without forcing a straight right edge.

The maths, the compromises, and what this demo doesn’t do +

This is a custom adaptation, not TeX in the browser. For a candidate line, let f be its natural width / measure, k its tracking advances × font size / measure, and t the added letter-spacing in em. We minimize:

C(t) = A(f + kt − target)² + Bt²
 t*  = clamp(Ak(target − f) / (Ak² + B), minTracking, maxTracking)

Other costs discourage short final lines, single-word endings, oversized words, and abrupt tracking changes. Ordinary lines pay a small line-count cost to discourage unnecessary breaks. No discretionary hyphens are introduced; Japanese uses Unicode break opportunities. Normal word-space widths are not independently stretched, although CSS letter-spacing also contributes advances around spaces.

The three paragraphs really are coupled. The document cost adds pairwise squared differences in mean body fill, four normalized body-contour samples, and final-line fill. Turning harmony to zero removes that coupling. We enumerate combinations of retained candidates for up to three paragraphs; larger documents use a 128-state document beam.

Approximate, deterministic search. Up to 32 diverse paths survive at each word boundary. This pruning keeps interaction fast but does not guarantee the global optimum. The weighted objective is a design hypothesis, not a validated aesthetic or readability score. “Ragged-edge deviation” excludes final lines; reducing it alone is not the goal.

Scope. Plain horizontal English, Japanese and Arabic. Japanese uses Unicode 13 line-break opportunities, not complete browser-specific Japanese tailoring. Arabic keeps connected font shaping and zero tracking. No inline markup, pagination, added hyphenation or optical margin alignment. Mixed-direction content is ordered by the browser within each explicit line; full paragraph-level bidi fidelity is not guaranteed. Single words wider than the measure are preserved and reported, not silently split. Short-ending protection is a soft penalty, not an absolute guarantee. Canvas/DOM agreement is tested in desktop Chrome; WebKit may retain Canvas ligatures and differ slightly. CSS text-wrap: pretty and balance are useful browser alternatives, but do not expose joint cross-paragraph scoring or per-line tracking.
